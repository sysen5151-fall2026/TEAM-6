Compare every way across Ithaca — then pick the green one that fits.

EcoCommute is a web app for an invited Cornell pilot group. Enter a start and a destination and it compares driving, carpool, bus, walking, biking and scooter for time and estimated CO2e, rules out what the weather or your limits make unreasonable, and recommends one with the reason. Confirm the trip you actually took and it earns badges and grows a shared campus tree.

The problem

People commute on habit, not on information.

Most students and employees pick how to travel without seeing what each option costs the planet, or whether walking, biking or the bus is actually reasonable today. Institutions want engagement evidence without exposing anyone’s routine.

🗺

See every way at once

One plan compares all ways between two places, using routed distance and time, so the trade-off is visible before you move.

💡

Honest, explained numbers

Each result shows its source and says the saving is an estimate, not verified avoided emissions.

🍃

Advice you can follow

Weather and time limits rule modes out first, and a student is assumed to have no bike until they say so. The choice always stays with you.

How it works

Plan first, then choose. Only confirmed trips count.

The main flow is use case UC.3 Plan and Choose a Trip in the Innoslate model. Every step maps to code and tests.

Sign in

An invited person creates an account with a shared invite code. Trips and progress are saved per account.

ADR 0009

Plan

Type a start and destination and pick from map-search suggestions. Routes for drive, bike and walk are drawn on the map.

UC.3.1–3.4 · ADR 0003

Compare and recommend

Every way gets distance, time and CO2e against driving. Weather and limits rule modes out; the lowest CO2e left wins, with the reason.

UC.3.5, 3.6 · ADR 0004

Choose

Pick the way you took, including which TCAT route or how many people shared the car. Repeated and excess trips are ignored.

UC.3.7, 3.8 · ADR 0007, 0008

Celebrate

The done page shows your estimated saving and any new badge. Garden and Achievements pages show campus and personal progress.

UC.3.9–3.11 · ADR 0005

Feasibility, not preaching

Rain rules out walk, bike and scooter; ice or strong wind rules out the wheeled ways; long trips pass the time limits. A student is assumed to have no bike until they tick one. Thresholds are project assumptions, labelled as such.

Carpool shares emissions

A carpool of 2 to 8 people splits the car’s emissions between them, so each person’s CO2e is the drive divided by the people. The saving is measured against driving alone.

Deterministic and explainable

The same inputs always give the same recommendation. The explanation states only facts from the calculated result; there is no language model in the loop yet.

Data sources

Real routes, weather and bus times — with labelled fallbacks.

Every external dependency is wrapped in the C.3 Data Adapter. When one is unavailable the page says so instead of guessing silently.

Live

OpenRouteService

Place search, autocomplete and drive, bike and walk routes. Needs a free API key; without one the page uses a labelled straight-line estimate between sample places. Looked-up places and routes are cached.

Live

Open-Meteo

Current precipitation, temperature and wind at the start, with no key. If the call fails the page says no weather rules were applied.

Built locally

TCAT GTFS schedule

Published bus schedule downloaded into a local index. Gives real routes, stops, times and when to leave for direct rides; times are the timetable, not live positions.

Progress

Eight published badges and one shared campus tree.

Badge rules are pure functions of your confirmed trips and are shown next to your progress. At most two trips per day count, a trip over 50 km counts as 50 km, and repeats are ignored. The numbers are project assumptions.

🌳 Campus tree

Every 22 kg of estimated difference from driving, added across all accounts, plants one tree in the animated Garden. Only the total is shown, never anyone’s trips.

Sample data is labelled

The campus share starts from invented sample trips and says so on the page, so a demo shows badges and trees at once without pretending they are real.

System of interest

Four internal components, a handful of outside services.

C.0 EcoCommute contains the web front end, the application service, the data adapter and the explanation model. The commuter and the outside services are external: the software controls its response, not people’s travel or the forecast.

Included

Plan and compare, feasibility and recommendation, TCAT direct rides, carpool, confirmed-trip history, badges, campus tree, invite-only accounts, data export and account deletion.

Excluded on purpose

Native apps, passive GPS tracking, carbon credits, live bus positions and multi-leg transit routing. Each would need new interfaces and review.

Privacy by design

Manual entry, no tracking. Your location stays in the browser until you press “Start from my location”. Passwords are salted scrypt hashes, and each account sees only its own trips.

Model ↔ product

Trace any behavior back to a stakeholder need.

Stakeholder → need → requirement → modeled action → code → test. A test reads the use-case actions from the exported Innoslate model and checks that every action a component performs has tagged code and that no other action is tagged.

Reviewed emission factors, a language-model explanation, live bus positions, multi-leg transit, and the pilot usability measurements (such as the 30-second entry target).

Estimates, not claims

Every saving is labelled an estimate. Emission factors, the tree goal and all feasibility limits are placeholders or project assumptions until the method reviewer supplies sources.

Provenance

GenAI-assisted work is logged in the prompt log, and code is constrained by the requirement text in SPEC.md. Decisions are recorded in ADR 0001–0009.
