How it works

Three steps. No tracking.

You tell it where you are going. It does the comparing. You stay in charge of the choice.

Plan

Type a start and a destination and pick from map suggestions. Routes for driving, biking and walking are drawn on the map, and the next TCAT buses are looked up.

Compare

Every way gets time, distance and estimated CO2e against driving alone. Rain, ice, strong wind and long walks rule options out first, and the recommendation says why.

Choose

Confirm the way you actually took. Only confirmed trips count toward your badges and the shared campus tree.

Progress

Small wins, visible progress.

Badge rules are public and are shown next to your progress. At most two trips a day count and repeats are ignored, so the numbers stay honest.

Honest by design

Numbers you can question.

EcoCommute gives decision support, not guarantees. Every result says where it came from and what it assumes.

  • Estimates, labelled as estimatesSavings are never presented as verified avoided emissions.
  • Sources on every resultRoutes from OpenRouteService, weather from Open-Meteo, buses from TCAT’s published schedule. When one is unavailable the page says so.
  • Same inputs, same answerThe recommendation follows fixed rules, not a language model, and states only facts from the calculation.
  • Factors still being reviewedEmission factors are placeholders until the method reviewer signs off on sources.
  • Invite-only accountsA shared invite code creates an account. Passwords are stored as salted hashes.
  • You see only your own tripsThe campus tree and totals add everyone up and show only the total.
  • Location stays in your browserNothing is shared until you press “Start from my location”. There is no passive tracking.
  • Export or delete anytimeDownload your data or delete your account from the My account page.

FAQ

Questions people ask.

Who can use EcoCommute?

It is an invite-only pilot for a small group at Cornell (the team, the professor and the TA, and invited testers). You need an invite code to create an account.

How is the recommendation chosen?

First it rules out ways the weather or time limits make unreasonable: rain for walking, biking and scooter; freezing or strong wind for biking and scooter; more than 30 minutes on foot or 45 on a bike or scooter. Of what is left, it recommends the lowest estimated CO2e, and ties go to walking, then biking, then scooter.

Are the CO2 numbers accurate?

They are estimates. The emission factors are placeholders for now and will be replaced by reviewed, sourced factors. The tree goal and the feasibility limits are project assumptions.

Is the bus real-time?

No. Bus times come from TCAT’s published timetable, for direct rides, not from live vehicle positions.

Do you track my location?

No. You type places or press a button to use your current position once. Your position stays in your browser.

Does it work outside Ithaca?

Place search and bus data are set up for the Ithaca area.

Who built this?

Team 6 for Cornell SYSEN 5151 (Foundations of Systems Engineering). See About the project for the requirements, architecture and status.

Want to try EcoCommute?

The app is invite-only during the Fall 2026 pilot. Ask a team member for an invite code, or follow the README to run it on your own computer.
