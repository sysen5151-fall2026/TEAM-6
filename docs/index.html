The problem

People commute on habit, not on information.

Most students and employees pick how to travel without any feedback on the environmental effect of that choice. There is no low-effort way to see a trip’s CO2e, or to know when walking or cycling is actually reasonable given distance and weather. Institutions, meanwhile, want engagement evidence without exposing anyone’s personal routine.

Fast to log

Suggestions use distance, forecast weather and your own “not feasible” settings. The choice always stays with you.

Operational concept

One primary use case, twelve modeled actions.

UC.1 Review Commute Impact and Feasible Alternative. Pick a step to see who performs it, the code that realizes it, and the requirements it satisfies. Every step exists in the Innoslate model and in the repository.

System of interest

What is inside the boundary — and what is not.

C.0 EcoCommute contains the interface, service, data adapter and explanation components. The commuter and the weather service are outside: we control the software’s response, not people’s travel or the forecast.

Included

Manual logging, documented CO2e estimates, weekly and monthly views, the always-drive baseline, explainable rules with feasibility controls, consent and account controls, approved institutional summaries.

Excluded on purpose

Native apps, passive GPS tracking, carbon credits and comprehensive transit integration. Adding any of them needs new external entities, interfaces and review.

Why this boundary

It keeps location exposure low, avoids a heavy supplier burden, and still lets a pilot test the real question: do commuters understand and act on the feedback?

Model ↔ product

Trace any behavior back to a stakeholder need.

Stakeholder → need → requirement → modeled action → code → test. A test reads the UC.1 actions from the exported Innoslate model and checks that each of the twelve has tagged code and that no other UC.1 action is tagged.

Not yet

Why Python

Standard library only, zero installs, and most of the team knows it. The stack decision is recorded in ADR 0001; the original Next.js/SQLite plan is deferred.

Provenance

GenAI-assisted work is logged in the prompt log, and code generation was constrained by the requirement text in SPEC.md rather than a broad prompt.
